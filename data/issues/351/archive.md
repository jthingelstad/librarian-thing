## Notable

### [The Incumbents Are Coming | Andreessen Horowitz](https://a16z.com/the-incumbents-are-coming/)

I find it really interesting to see how established platforms and systems of record are evolving around AI. The most personal example for me is AWS. I've used AWS for years but frankly I find it nearly impossible to work myself. I stumbled around with it and get something working and then never touch it. Today, my AWS account is completely managed with code and better than it ever has been. How? Claude Code helped me revamp it. AWS has realized that its customer is the Agent as much as anyone else, and has started building to it. In many ways we are seeing a new layer of capability being built either on top of platforms or systems-of-record. The right move for those companies may be to just capture the core value and allow those to build on top? At the same time, those platform companies can be a little more measured and build where their signals tell them there is real value to provide the market.

### [The Overhang - Ethan Mollick](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-overhang)

Mollick continues to approach AI from a pragmatic, center-of-the-road position. In general I am an "applied" technologist. A builder. So I tend to push the most on what we can do now and in the near future. I think Mollick's reminder that even if all progress stopped in AI there is still a huge transformation in front of us.

> My point is that, while there is a lot of debate over what future models will do, the current capabilities of existing models are barely being used, and are often not even well understood.

I think his highlight of the uniquely human things that make our partnership with agents the most powerful is a good callout on where to focus:

1. Deep knowledge: the expertise that comes from understanding a field or subject so well that you build intuition around it to quickly and accurately make decisions.
2. Wide knowledge: To gain wide knowledge you need to read and study widely, across fields and formats and traditions.
3. Taste: Some people have a taste for things that many people will find popular, others have a taste that is unique to them, and still others have a taste for what is novel and new.
4. Agency: willingness to test the boundaries of what’s possible when everybody is equally confused about what AI can do.

It is notable to me that everything described there is what would make a great **Product Engineer**.

### [M5 Ultra Mac Studio Review: The Dream Mac for Local AI Agents - MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/m5-ultra-mac-studio-review-the-dream-mac-for-local-ai-agents/)

The local LLM capabilities of these new M5 Ultras are incredible. I'd also nudge you to this [Apple Wasn't Messing Around](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c_58D7ixOQI) video for a great walk through as well. Two thoughts come to mind with this.

First, Apple has a unique position here with their M-series chips. They are able to deliver more processing power per watt than anybody else. Sure that matters to keep data center cooling and power lower, but the even bigger thing is the ability to put LLM processing power on a desktop that doesn't require special environmental needs. You can see a direct path from an M5 Ultra to an AI Appliance that people could buy and put in their house.

The second is a direct path to local models and product engineering. A long time ago software developers used to get the fastest and biggest computers around because compilers were taxing, and building software took a long time. These days most developers need nothing but a simple laptop as the power of those machines has gone up and the needs have gone down with the cloud and interpreted software stacks. Now as we enter AI-assisted coding we may be flipping back to a world where a developer can justify a $50,000 workstation to run models and build software cheaper. It is also interesting to consider a completely verticalized stack where the development environment is completely merged into a custom model. Instead of plugging a frontier model on the side, you are natively connected to a specific model.

Both of these seem like nearly certain developments in the next three years.

### [I am often wrong | Boris Cherny's Blog](https://borischerny.com/management,/product/2026/09/19/I-am-often-wrong.html)

This is mostly notable because it is from the author of Claude Code and I think his "thinking loop" is worth noting.

> 1. Understand the available information
> 2. Gather missing information
> 3. Define the problem
> 4. Define a clear and simple approach to solve the problem
> 5. Define a goal
> 6. Act with urgency to achieve the goal

Funny enough, I recently did an extended session with Claude Code using a thinking loop with execution in the middle to move a project forward. Very similar to what Cherny describes.

### [AI is too important to fear, too powerful to use carelessly](https://www.startribune.com/artificial-intelligence-anthropic-open-ai-jacob-coxon/601890526)

Great letter to the editor from [MnTech](https://mntech.org) CEO [Joel Crandall](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joel-d-crandall/). I think we will see some form of AI legislation, if not at the Federal level at the State level, and technology companies need to be thinking about that now. MnTech is getting ahead of that. Data centers, privacy, AI — this is all going to blend together and companies need to engage, as do voters.

### [Attention is all you have](https://alicegg.tech/2026/09/21/attention)

I think attention is perhaps one of the most important things that we don't think about enough. It is the thing that I think meditation is most helpful for.

> There’s one lesson the Tetris effect teaches us: whatever you focus on long enough will end up shaping your thoughts. This can be a good thing since it’s how we learn new skills and discover new ideas. Sadly, less and less of our attention is focused intentionally. Instead of picking what we want to see we let other people decide what is supposed to be good for us.

For me this shows up when I'm meditating and I find my mind constantly returning to a topic. That is the lens of my attention.

This is also why I never allow algorithms to shape what I choose to read and engage in. That is synonymous with your attention, and I don't think we should **ever** allow something that we don't control to shape our attention.
